🌟 What is this system?

The Village Pond Planning System is an advanced hydrological tool designed to help civil engineers and village administrators find the perfect location to construct a rainwater harvesting pond.

Instead of relying on guesswork, the system uses physical terrain data, historical climate patterns, and satellite APIs to mathematically prove where a pond will capture the most water.

🗺️ Terrain Intelligence

Extracts 3D elevation grids directly from your KML/KMZ contour maps to find natural depressions.

🚫 Smart Exclusion

Connects to OpenStreetMap (OSM) to automatically prevent building dams on existing rivers, lakes, or residential housing.

🌧️ Climate Integration

Fetches 10-year historical rainfall data from NASA POWER and Open-Meteo APIs for exact catchment coordinates.

📐 IS 5477 Compliance

Designs the pond dimensions (depth, width, embankments) strictly following Indian standard civil engineering codes.

🖱️ How to Use It

There are two ways to start an analysis. Both return the same results:

Option A — Upload a KMZ Contour File

1

Upload your Terrain Data

Drag and drop a .kml or .kmz file containing contour lines into the sidebar. The system requires accurate topography to work. This is the highest-precision input method (~1–2 m resolution).

2

Wait for AI Analysis (~60–90 s)

The backend builds a 3D terrain model, simulates water flow, excludes built-up lands, fetches satellite weather data, and designs the pond.

3

Review the Optimal Site (★)

The map centers on the best pond location. The sidebar populates with full engineering metrics.

4

Manual Override (Optional)

Click "Click map to override" and click any map location. The system instantly recalculates for that point.

Option B — Draw an Area on the Map

1

Click "🗺 Draw Area on Map"

Your cursor changes to a crosshair. You can draw a rectangle anywhere in the world — no file upload needed.

2

Click and Drag a Rectangle

While dragging, a live area counter appears. Green = valid size. Red = too large (max 10 sq km = 1,000 ha, configurable). Release the mouse to confirm.

3

Click "▶ Analyze Selected Area"

The backend automatically downloads free SRTM satellite elevation tiles for your rectangle from AWS, builds a DEM, and runs the full analysis pipeline.

4

Results + Reference Boundary

All results appear in the sidebar. Your original drawn rectangle stays visible on the map as a dashed blue boundary for reference, showing exactly what area was analyzed.

⚙️ How It Works (The Engine)

When you upload a file, a complex multi-stage pipeline runs on the server:

A

DEM Generation & Conditioning

The KML contours are interpolated into a 2-meter resolution Digital Elevation Model (DEM). The Priority-Flood algorithm is applied to simulate how water fills the terrain.

B

Flow Routing (D8) & Sinks

The system calculates Flow Direction and Flow Accumulation (using the D8 method) to find where water naturally pools into "sinks" (depressions).

C

Catchment Delineation

Once the best depression is found, a Breadth-First Search (BFS) traces upstream to map the exact watershed boundary (the "Catchment Area") that feeds water to this pond.

D

SCS-CN Runoff Estimation

Using 10-year average rainfall from NASA, the system applies the USDA SCS-CN (Curve Number) method. This calculates exactly how much rain gets absorbed by the soil vs. how much becomes usable runoff.

E

Pond Dimensioning (IS 5477)

Finally, the algorithm sizes the physical dam. It calculates Live Storage, Dead Storage (for silt), and Evaporation Freeboard to guarantee the pond won't dry up in summer or overflow in monsoon.

📊 Understanding the Results

Here is what the numbers in the sidebar actually mean:

Metric Meaning
Catchment Area The total land area (in hectares) uphill from the pond. Every drop of rain that falls in this zone will flow down into your pond.
Runoff Depth The amount of rainfall that actually flows over the ground (after accounting for soil soaking it up and evaporation).
Live Storage The actual volume of usable water (in cubic meters) the pond will hold for village use.
Total Storage The total volume of the pond, which includes the Live Storage + Dead Storage (space for mud/silt to settle) + Freeboard (safety height).
Topographic Capacity The maximum physical amount of water the natural terrain bowl could hold before overflowing its rim.
Design Constrained By If this says hydrology, it means we limited the pond size because there isn't enough rain to fill a bigger one. If it says topography, it means there is plenty of rain, but the physical hole in the ground isn't big enough!

📡 Data Sources & Accuracy

This system is built entirely on free, publicly available data. Here is what each data source provides and its known limitations:

Data Source Accuracy / Limitation
Elevation (KMZ path) User-supplied KMZ contour map ~1–2 m resolution. Highest precision. Best for final engineering design.
Elevation (Draw Area path) AWS Terrarium Tiles (SRTM-derived, free, no key) ~38 m/pixel at zoom 12. Suitable for regional site selection. Not recommended for precise embankment placement.
Rainfall Open-Meteo ERA5-Land (primary) → NASA POWER MERRA-2 (fallback) ~9 km and ~50 km resolution respectively. 10-year historical average. Free, no key required.
Water bodies & land use OpenStreetMap via Overpass API Crowd-sourced. Coverage varies by region — excellent in India, gaps possible in very rural areas. System is fail-open: if unavailable, no exclusion is applied.
Satellite basemap Esri World Imagery / CartoDB / OSM Display only. Not used in any calculation.
